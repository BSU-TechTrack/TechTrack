ABSTRACT

Scholarly Overview

"The increasing use of technology in higher education has significantly influenced how students learn, communicate, and accomplish academic tasks. This study investigates the impact of technology use on the academic performance of first-year Computer Engineering students at Batangas State University – Alangilan Campus."

The rapid shift toward digital education has transformed higher learning in the Philippines, positioning technology as both an essential academic enabler and a pervasive cognitive hazard. This comprehensive study examines how digital devices, screen-time volume, mobile notifications, and artificial intelligence interact with student academic performance in college environments. Synthesizing empirical findings from Philippine state universities, regional PSA data, and international OECD benchmarks, this paper maps the bifurcated realities of modern learners who must balance technical velocity against continuous attentional fragmentation.
